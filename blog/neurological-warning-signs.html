Neurological Warning Signs You Shouldn't Dismiss

Sudden severe headache unlike any before, new numbness or weakness on one side of the body, slurred speech, sudden vision changes, or repeated dizziness and fainting all deserve prompt medical attention — some of these can signal an emergency.

Signs that need urgent attention

  • Sudden, severe headache unlike any you've had before
  • New numbness or weakness on one side of the face, arm, or leg
  • Slurred or difficult speech, or trouble understanding others
  • Sudden vision loss or double vision
  • Repeated dizziness, loss of balance, or fainting

These symptoms can be early signs of a stroke, where every minute matters. If they appear suddenly, call SAMU (114) immediately rather than waiting to see if they pass.

Signs worth an unhurried assessment

Not everything neurological is an emergency. Recurring headaches, tingling or pins-and-needles in the hands or feet, occasional dizziness, or memory concerns that are starting to affect daily life are all worth mentioning at a consultation — even if they've been going on for a while and don't feel urgent.

What happens at a consultation

An internal medicine consultation typically starts with a detailed history and a focused neurological examination, which often narrows things down considerably before any tests are needed. From there, it's a question of determining what's happening and whether further specialist referral — to a neurologist, for instance — is needed, or whether the issue can be managed and monitored directly.
