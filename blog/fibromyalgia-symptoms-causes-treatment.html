Pain Everywhere and Tired All the Time? Fibromyalgia Explained

Do you ache all over, with pain that moves from your neck to your back, hips and legs? Do you wake up exhausted even after a full night in bed? Is your memory foggy and your concentration poor? Have you had blood tests, X-rays and scans, only to be told that everything is “normal”? This could be fibromyalgia. It is real, it is common, and it can be managed.

What Is Fibromyalgia?

Fibromyalgia is a long-term condition of widespread pain, together with fatigue, unrefreshing sleep and problems with memory and concentration. It is not caused by damage to the joints or muscles. The problem lies in the way the nervous system processes pain: the brain and spinal cord become over-sensitive and “turn up the volume” on pain signals, so that normal sensations can hurt. Doctors call this central sensitisation.

It affects roughly 2 to 4 in every 100 adults, most often women between 30 and 60, although men and younger people can also be affected.

The Warning Signs

  • Widespread pain, on both sides of the body, above and below the waist, lasting more than 3 months.
  • Deep tiredness that rest does not relieve.
  • Poor, light or broken sleep, waking unrefreshed.
  • “Fibro fog”: trouble concentrating, finding words or remembering things.
  • Morning stiffness, headaches and tingling in the hands or feet.
  • Digestive and bladder symptoms, such as bloating, irregular bowels or an irritable bladder.
  • Low mood, anxiety and sensitivity to noise, light or temperature.

What Causes It?

The exact cause is not known, but several factors can trigger it or make it worse:

  • Long-term stress or an emotional shock.
  • Poor sleep over a long period, including untreated sleep apnoea.
  • An infection, surgery or accident.
  • Other painful conditions, such as arthritis or chronic back pain.
  • Family history: it can run in families, although it is not directly inherited.

Is It Really Fibromyalgia? How It Is Diagnosed

There is no blood test, X-ray or scan that confirms fibromyalgia. The diagnosis is made by a doctor from your story and an examination, using established criteria: widespread pain for more than 3 months, together with fatigue, unrefreshing sleep and thinking problems.

Tests are still useful, because other conditions can look similar and some of them are treatable. Your doctor may check for:

  • Thyroid disease, anaemia and vitamin D deficiency.
  • Inflammatory arthritis or lupus, which are different from osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis and need their own treatment.
  • Muscle disease, which can cause pain and weakness.
  • Sleep apnoea and depression, which are common and can coexist with fibromyalgia.

Normal results do not mean that your pain is imaginary. They mean that your joints and muscles are not being damaged, which is good news.

What Happens If It Is Left Untreated?

Fibromyalgia does not damage joints, deform the body or shorten life. But if it is ignored, the cycle of pain, poor sleep, stress and inactivity can deepen, leading to exhaustion, low mood and difficulty working or looking after the family. Early understanding and treatment make a real difference.

How Is It Treated?

There is no single magic pill. The best results come from combining several approaches:

  • Understanding the condition. Knowing that the pain is real but not dangerous reduces fear, and fear itself makes pain worse.
  • Regular, gentle exercise. This is the most effective treatment we have. Walking, swimming or exercising in water, stretching, yoga and tai chi are good choices. Start with 5 to 10 minutes and build up slowly. Some soreness at first is common and usually settles.
  • Better sleep. Keep fixed bedtimes and wake-up times, avoid screens in bed, limit caffeine in the afternoon, and keep the room cool and dark.
  • Stress and mind-body support. Relaxation, breathing exercises, mindfulness and cognitive behavioural therapy can reduce pain and improve daily function.
  • Medication, when needed. Some medicines that act on nerve pain pathways can help certain patients, but they are chosen individually by your doctor. Ordinary painkillers and anti-inflammatories usually do little, and strong opioids are not recommended.

Myths and Facts

  • “It is all in your head.” No. Pain processing in the brain and spinal cord is genuinely different in fibromyalgia.
  • “Rest is best.” No. Too much rest increases pain and stiffness. Gentle regular movement helps.
  • “It is destroying my joints.” No. It is painful, but it does not damage or deform joints or muscles.
  • “Nothing can be done.” No. Most people improve with a structured plan.

When to Seek Medical Help Quickly

  • 🔴 Chest pain, breathlessness or sweating: call SAMU (114) or go to hospital immediately. Do not assume it is “just fibromyalgia”.
  • 🔴 Thoughts of harming yourself, or feeling that you cannot go on: seek help immediately from a doctor, a loved one or the emergency services.
  • 🔴 A hot, red, swollen joint, especially with fever.
  • 🟡 Weight loss, fever, a rash, or marked muscle weakness, which are not typical of fibromyalgia and need investigation.
  • 🟡 Swollen joints or morning stiffness lasting more than an hour, which may point to inflammatory arthritis.
  • 🟡 Pain and exhaustion lasting more than 3 months, or affecting your work, sleep or family life.

The Bottom Line

Fibromyalgia is common, genuine and very much treatable. It is not a sign of weakness, and it is not something you have to simply put up with. With the right diagnosis, gentle regular exercise, better sleep and support, most people feel clearly better and return to the activities they enjoy.

If you have had widespread pain and tiredness for months and no one has been able to explain it, do not give up. Always consult your doctor before starting or stopping any medicine.
