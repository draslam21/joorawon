Numb, Tingling Hands at Night? Carpal Tunnel Syndrome Explained

Do you wake at 3 a.m. with fingers that are numb, burning or “asleep”, and find yourself shaking your hand to get the feeling back? Do you drop your cup, struggle with buttons or lose your grip on the steering wheel? Were you told it is “just tiredness” or “the way you sleep”? This could be carpal tunnel syndrome, the most common nerve compression problem in the body. The good news is that it is very treatable, especially when it is recognised early.

What Is Carpal Tunnel Syndrome?

The carpal tunnel is a narrow channel at the base of your palm, formed by the wrist bones and a tough ligament across the top. The median nerve, which gives feeling to part of the hand and powers some of the thumb muscles, runs through it together with the tendons that bend your fingers. When the tunnel becomes crowded, because of swelling, thickened tissue or a change in wrist position, the nerve is squeezed. The result is tingling, numbness and, if the pressure continues, weakness.

The Warning Signs

  • Numbness, tingling or burning in the thumb, index finger, middle finger and half of the ring finger. The little finger is spared, which is a useful clue.
  • Night symptoms, waking you from sleep, often eased by shaking or rubbing the hand (doctors call this the “flick sign”).
  • Symptoms during activity, such as driving, holding a phone, typing, knitting or gripping a book.
  • Clumsiness and a weaker grip, dropping objects, difficulty with buttons or jar lids.
  • Pain or an ache in the wrist that sometimes spreads up the forearm.
  • Later signs: constant numbness, and thinning of the muscle at the base of the thumb. These suggest the nerve is under significant strain.

Who Gets It, and Why?

Often there is no single cause. Several things make it more likely:

  • Being a woman, aged 40 to 60. The tunnel tends to be smaller in women, and hormonal changes play a part.
  • Pregnancy, because of fluid retention. It often settles after delivery.
  • Diabetes. High blood sugar makes nerves more vulnerable to compression. See also the warning signs of diabetes.
  • An underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism).
  • Inflammatory arthritis, such as rheumatoid arthritis, and also gout and kidney disease.
  • Excess weight, a previous wrist fracture or wrist injury.
  • Hand work: forceful, repetitive gripping, awkward wrist positions and vibrating tools. Ordinary keyboard and mouse use is much less clearly linked than many people believe.
  • Family history: it often runs in families.

Is It Really Carpal Tunnel Syndrome? How It Is Diagnosed

Numb hands have many causes, including a pinched nerve in the neck (see neck pain and cervicalgia), a nerve compressed at the elbow, nerve damage from diabetes or other diseases, arthritis at the base of the thumb and, occasionally, vitamin deficiencies. Diagnosis usually combines:

  • Your story and an examination. Where the numbness is, what brings it on, and the strength and sensation of the hand. Tapping over the nerve at the wrist (Tinel’s test) or holding the wrist bent for a minute (Phalen’s test) may reproduce the symptoms.
  • Blood tests, when needed, to look for diabetes, thyroid disease, kidney problems or inflammation.
  • A nerve conduction study or a wrist ultrasound, used when the diagnosis is uncertain, when symptoms are severe or not improving, or before surgery.

A useful clue: if the little finger is also numb, or the problem starts in the neck, carpal tunnel syndrome may not be the whole explanation.

What Happens If It Is Left Untreated?

In the early stage, symptoms come and go and the nerve recovers fully. If the squeeze continues for months or years, the nerve can be damaged: numbness becomes constant, the grip weakens, the muscle at the base of the thumb wastes, and recovery after treatment can be slow or incomplete. This is why persistent symptoms deserve assessment rather than waiting.

How Is It Treated?

  • A wrist splint at night. It keeps the wrist straight so the nerve is not squeezed while you sleep. The evidence for a small, safe, inexpensive benefit is modest, and it works best in mild to moderate cases and early on.
  • Changing what aggravates it. Avoid sustained wrist bending, prolonged gripping and vibrating tools; take regular breaks; and adjust your workstation and sleeping position.
  • Treating the underlying condition. Controlling diabetes, correcting a thyroid problem, treating arthritis or losing weight can improve or even resolve the symptoms. If you are pregnant, symptoms often settle after delivery.
  • Corticosteroid injection into the carpal tunnel, or sometimes a short course of steroid tablets. It can give good relief for weeks to months, but symptoms may return. In a large trial, injection and night splints gave similar results over two years.
  • Surgery (carpal tunnel release) cuts the ligament to give the nerve room. It is usually a short procedure under local anaesthetic, with no need to stay in hospital, and the results are generally very good. It is recommended when symptoms persist despite proper treatment, or when there is constant numbness, weakness, muscle wasting or nerve damage on testing. After surgery, gentle early movement is encouraged rather than immobilising the wrist.

Some people try hand exercises, nerve-gliding exercises, supplements or acupuncture. Evidence for these is limited, so discuss them with your doctor rather than relying on them alone.

Habits That Help

  • Keep your wrists in a neutral, straight position when typing, writing and sleeping.
  • Take short breaks every 30 to 60 minutes from repetitive or gripping tasks, and shake out and stretch your hands.
  • Use a light grip, and avoid squeezing a phone, pen or tool harder than you need to.
  • Keep your blood sugar, thyroid and weight in check.
  • Do not ignore symptoms that wake you at night: early treatment gives the best results.

When to Seek Medical Help Quickly

  • 🔴 Sudden numbness or weakness of the face, arm or leg, difficulty speaking or confusion: this may be a stroke. Call SAMU (114) or go to hospital immediately.
  • 🔴 A hot, red, swollen and very painful wrist, especially with fever: a joint or tendon infection must be ruled out urgently.
  • 🔴 Severe wrist pain and numbness after an injury or fracture: the nerve may be under acute pressure.
  • 🟡 Weakness in the hand or thinning at the base of the thumb, or numbness that is now constant.
  • 🟡 Night numbness lasting more than a few weeks, or symptoms in both hands, especially with tiredness, weight gain, thirst or joint swelling.
  • 🟡 You are pregnant, have diabetes or take blood thinners, and want to know which treatments are safe for you.

The Bottom Line

Carpal tunnel syndrome is common, uncomfortable and very treatable. A night splint and simple changes are enough for many people, an injection can help a flare, and surgery gives a reliable fix when the nerve needs more space. The sooner it is assessed, the better the nerve recovers.

If your hands are waking you at night, losing their grip or staying numb, do not wait for it to go away on its own. Always consult your doctor before starting or stopping any medicine.
